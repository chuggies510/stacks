### Claims

- In a three-party introduction thread, the introduced party's reply does double duty: it must accept the new contact's handoff AND thank the introducer by name in the same short message, because the introducer is cc'd and will see it. [source: exemplar-referral-acknowledgment-email, line ~50]
- Open the reply with thanks and a brief warmth line ("Great to be connected") before moving to logistics. [source: exemplar-referral-acknowledgment-email, line ~53]
- Accept a scheduling handoff with a concrete next step tied to the mechanism the other party offered (e.g. "I will find a spot on your calendar" against a scheduling link), not a vague "I'll be in touch." [source: exemplar-referral-acknowledgment-email, line ~55]
- The introducer's hand-off states the connection and the fit in one sentence each, names the concrete next step (reach out directly), and cc's the person being introduced so the two can connect. [source: exemplar-referral-acknowledgment-email, lines ~15-23, 58]
- The recipient of a referral should hand over a concrete scheduling mechanism (a link) rather than trading open-ended availability back and forth. [source: exemplar-referral-acknowledgment-email, lines ~25-30, 60]
- Keep sign-offs short; a first-name-with-a-dash greeting ("Hi [Name]-") carries the warm register in this exchange, consistent with the professional-courtesy email register. [source: exemplar-referral-acknowledgment-email, line ~62]
- Contrast pair: an earlier terse draft reply ("Thank you. I will fill out a spot.") fails on two counts — "fill out a spot" is a broken idiom (you fill out a *form*; you find or grab a *spot*), and the draft thanks no one, omitting the introducer who did the favor and is watching the thread as a cc'd party. [source: exemplar-referral-acknowledgment-email, lines ~38-44]
