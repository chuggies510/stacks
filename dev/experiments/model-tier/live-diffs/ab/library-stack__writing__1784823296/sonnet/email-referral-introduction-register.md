## Overview

Accepting a warm email introduction is a three-party exchange: a mutual contact hands two people off to each other by email, cc'ing both, and the reply from the introduced party has to work on two fronts at once [exemplar-referral-acknowledgment-email].

## Key Concepts

In a three-party introduction thread, the introduced party's reply must do double duty: it has to accept the new contact's handoff AND thank the introducer by name in the same short message, because the introducer is cc'd and will see it [exemplar-referral-acknowledgment-email]. The hand-off that starts the thread sets this up — the introducer states the connection and the fit in one sentence each, names the concrete next step (reach out directly), and cc's the person being introduced so the two can connect [exemplar-referral-acknowledgment-email].

## Patterns

- Open the reply with thanks and a brief warmth line ("Great to be connected") before moving to logistics [exemplar-referral-acknowledgment-email].
- Accept a scheduling handoff with a concrete next step tied to the mechanism the other party offered ("I will find a spot on your calendar" against a scheduling link), not a vague "I'll be in touch" [exemplar-referral-acknowledgment-email]. This is the same concrete-next-step move used in [[email-internal-work-request-register|internal work-request emails]].
- The recipient of a referral should hand over a concrete scheduling mechanism (a link) rather than trading open-ended availability back and forth [exemplar-referral-acknowledgment-email].
- Keep sign-offs short; a first-name-with-a-dash greeting ("Hi [Name]-") carries the warm register in this exchange, consistent with the [[email-professional-courtesy-register|professional-courtesy email register]] [exemplar-referral-acknowledgment-email].

## Examples

A terse early draft of the introduced party's reply read: "Thank you. I will fill out a spot." [exemplar-referral-acknowledgment-email]. It fails on two counts: "fill out a spot" is a broken idiom (you fill out a *form*; you find or grab a *spot*), and the draft thanks no one, omitting the introducer who did the favor and is watching the thread as a cc'd party [exemplar-referral-acknowledgment-email].

The revised reply fixes both: "Hi [Recruiter]- Thank you. Great to be connected. I will find a spot on your calendar. [Introducer], thank you for the introduction! Sincerely," [exemplar-referral-acknowledgment-email].
